# Main Results

> Executive summary of the project's empirical findings, written for non-statisticians. Numbers are auto-generated by `Rscript R/03_analyse.R` into [outputs/tables/headline.csv](outputs/tables/headline.csv). Figures by `Rscript R/04_visualise.R`.

## What we measure

For each of Portugal's 39 mainland Unidades Locais de Saúde (ULS):

$$\text{Mobility}(uls, t) = \text{HospitalDeliveries}(uls, t) - \text{ResidentBirths}(uls, t)$$

- **HospitalDeliveries**: deliveries reported by SNS hospitals geographically inside that ULS (Transparência SNS).
- **ResidentBirths**: live births to mothers residing in that ULS (PORDATA aggregated by concelho via [`ulsportugal`](https://github.com/danielrodriguescode/ulsportugal); 3 split concelhos — Lisboa, Loures, Porto — allocated proportionally to freguesia counts).
- **Mobility > 0** ⇒ the ULS absorbs deliveries from outside its catchment (net importer).
- **Mobility < 0** ⇒ residents deliver elsewhere (net exporter — to other ULS, private hospitals, at home, or abroad).

The previous draft used a capacity-weighted *expected* formula (Observed − TotalBirths × Capacity / Σ Capacity); we replaced it because using historical deliveries as a "capacity" proxy is circular and tends to zero out the very magnetos we want to detect.

## Plain-language reading

1. **Most ULS are net exporters** — their residents deliver outside the ULS catchment more often than non-residents come in. Across all 39 ULS the mean per-ULS deficit is 434 deliveries per year. This is consistent with two effects: (a) genuine cross-ULS mobility (referrals, perceived quality, proximity), and (b) the SNS-vs-private coverage gap — PORDATA includes births in private hospitals while Transparência SNS does not.

2. **Coimbra is the strongest magnet in Portugal** — ULS Coimbra delivered an average of 4,742 babies/year between 2014–2024 against just 2,529 resident births. **+87 %** of its deliveries come from outside its catchment.

3. **Other clear magnetos**: ULS Santo António (+787/year), ULS Braga (+509), ULS Matosinhos (+158), ULS Viseu Dão-Lafões (+154), ULS Baixo Alentejo (+78). All are mid-sized referral hospitals with strong reputations or geographic centrality.

4. **The largest exporters are in the Lisbon metropolitan area**:
   - ULS Amadora/Sintra: −3,280 deliveries/year (only 45 % of resident births stay)
   - ULS Lisboa Ocidental: −1,841 (only 56 % stay)
   - ULS Loures-Odivelas, Almada-Seixal, Estuário do Tejo: all around −650
   - This is consistent with private maternity provision being concentrated in the same metro area.

5. **ULS Barcelos/Esposende has 0 SNS deliveries**: structurally — the ULS does not operate a maternity service, so its residents are referred to ULS Braga. This explains both the perfect outflow at Barcelos (−1,195) and a chunk of Braga's inflow.

6. **H2 is non-significant** (p = 0.48). The 6 urban tertiary ULS span the entire mobility distribution: ULS Coimbra is the biggest magneto (+2,213), but ULS Lisboa Ocidental is among the biggest exporters (−1,841). The "urban tertiary" label is too coarse — Lisbon ULS are structurally undersized vs their resident populations regardless of academic status.

7. **Spatial clustering is significant but weak** (Moran's I = 0.115, p = 0.034). Adjacent ULS tend to have similar mobility — magnetos cluster (Porto + Braga + Matosinhos + Tâmega-Sousa form a corridor) and so do exporters (the Lisbon ring). This is the cleanest causal-spatial finding and is robust to the SNS coverage gap.

8. **Mobility is drifting downward at ~7.5 deliveries/ULS/year** (p = 0.032) — the gap between resident births and SNS deliveries is widening, consistent with continued growth of private-sector maternity care.

## Statistical tests

| Test | Hypothesis | Statistic | p | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One-sample t-test (H1) | Mean ULS mobility ≠ 0 | t = −3.42 (df 38) | 0.001 | **Reject H₀** — mean is negative |
| Wilcoxon (H2) | Urban tertiary mobility > peripheral | W = 80 | 0.481 | Cannot reject H₀ — direction not even consistent (some urban tertiaries are magnets, others exporters) |
| `lmer(mobility ~ year + (1\|uls))` (H3) | Year coefficient ≠ 0 | β = −7.5, t = −2.16 | 0.032 | **Reject H₀** — gap widening |
| Moran's I (H4) | Spatial clustering of mean mobility | I = 0.115 | 0.034 | **Reject H₀** — significant but modest spatial autocorrelation |

## Limitations

- **SNS coverage gap (~13–20 % of continental births)** — PORDATA counts all live births to residents; Transparência SNS counts only deliveries at public hospitals. Private maternity provision is heavily concentrated in Lisboa, Porto and the Algarve, which explains a substantial share of the negative mobility in those metro areas. The H4 spatial-clustering finding is robust to this; the magnitude of H1 is not.
- **PPP hospitals (Cascais, Loures, Braga, Vila Franca de Xira)** are excluded from the main analysis (B-i decision). They are SNS-funded but operationally separate and have no defined ULS catchment in `ulsportugal`. Their hospital-level deliveries are reported in [outputs/tables/ppp_panel.csv](outputs/tables/ppp_panel.csv) and on the dashboard's "PPP hospitals" tab.
- **3 split concelhos (Lisboa, Loures, Porto)** allocated proportionally to freguesia count per ULS — better would be freguesia-level population weights (not currently in the project).
- **Hospital identity drift (CHU → ULS rename in 2024)** is resolved by spatial point-in-polygon for hospitals not exactly named after a ULS; the 2 hospitals with imprecise lat/lng (Amadora/Sintra and Santa Maria, both reported as longitude −9.0) are matched by direct ULS name match.
- **ULS Barcelos/Esposende** has 0 SNS deliveries because it does not operate a maternity service — its residents are referred to ULS Braga. This is correctly captured as 100 % outflow but should be flagged in the paper.
- **No risk adjustment** — high-risk pregnancies are referred to academic centres (ULS Coimbra, ULS Santa Maria, ULS São João) for clinical reasons. That's "legitimate" mobility that wouldn't be solved by data portability.

## Policy implication

Whether the missing patients in Lisbon are in another region, in a private hospital, or in their own home, the prenatal records held by the regional public provider are not where the delivery is happening. The fact that **Coimbra absorbs 87 % of its deliveries from outside** and that **Amadora/Sintra residents only deliver 45 % of the time inside their ULS** is direct evidence that obstetric care in Portugal is geographically dispersed across institutional boundaries. **The SNS needs portable, interoperable maternal health records — not as a convenience, but as a clinical safety requirement.**
